Executive Summary

Potholes are a persistent hazard on urban and highway roads, especially for two-wheelers, rickshaws and public transport. Today they are found mostly through manual inspection or scattered public complaints, which are slow, inconsistently located, and rarely tracked through to a confirmed repair.

SmartPothole AI proposes an end-to-end platform that closes this gap. A rider mounts an Android phone on the dashboard; a quantized YOLOv8 model detects potholes on the device in real time, warns the driver by voice and vibration, and sends only a small JSON record — class, confidence, GPS position and time — to a Firebase backend. No camera image or video ever leaves the phone. Reports from many vehicles are merged into one record per physical pothole, and a role-based web dashboard lets the City Corporation triage, assign, and finally close each repair, while contractors see and update only their own jobs.

A working prototype already exists: the Android app, web dashboard, database security rules and AI export pipeline are implemented, and the core web workflow and 17 security-rule tests pass on Firebase emulators. This proposal requests support to complete the remaining work over 16 weeks: on-device validation, a locally collected and labelled dataset, model retraining and evaluation, server-side notifications, and a small field pilot with measurable outcomes.

1Background and Problem Statement

Road surface damage grows quickly after heavy rain and heavy traffic. A pothole that is not repaired early causes vehicle damage, sudden braking and swerving, and accidents — with the greatest risk to motorcycles, bicycles and rickshaws. Municipal bodies are responsible for repair but usually lack an up-to-date, accurate picture of where defects are and how serious they are.

Gaps in current practice

  • Slow, manual discovery. Defects are found by periodic inspection or by complaints that arrive late and with vague locations.
  • No single source of truth. The same pothole is reported many times by different people, while others are never reported.
  • Weak accountability. Once a job is handed to a contractor, there is often no auditable record of who verified, repaired and approved it.
  • Privacy and cost barriers. Uploading road video from many phones is expensive in bandwidth and storage and captures people and number plates.
  • No driver-side benefit. Existing reporting tools rarely warn the driver about a hazard ahead.

Problem statement. There is no low-cost, privacy-preserving system that automatically detects potholes from ordinary vehicles, de-duplicates the reports into reliable geolocated records, and manages each defect through an auditable repair workflow between citizens, the city authority and contractors.

2Aim and Objectives

Aim: to design, build and evaluate a smartphone-based, on-device AI system for pothole detection that is connected to a secure, role-based civic road-maintenance workflow.

Specific objectives

  1. Run a lightweight YOLOv8 pothole detector in real time on mid-range Android phones, with no image upload.
  2. Reduce false and duplicate reports through temporal confirmation, confidence and spatial gates, and fresh-GPS requirements.
  3. Warn drivers immediately, offline, with voice and haptic alerts that are aware of travel direction.
  4. Merge detections from many vehicles into one record per physical pothole using concurrency-safe transactions.
  5. Provide a role-gated web dashboard and a nine-stage, fully audited repair lifecycle enforced by database rules.
  6. Collect and label a local road dataset, retrain the model, and report precision, recall, mAP and on-device latency.
  7. Validate the complete system in a small field pilot and document the results.

3Proposed Solution

The platform has three parts connected through a serverless, real-time backend. Intelligence sits at the edge (the phone), authority sits in the database security rules, and each human role gets a scoped interface.

Figure 1. System architecture. Arrows carry structured data only; camera frames never leave the phone.

Key features

  • Edge AI with zero image upload: detection, severity and warnings run on the phone; only coordinates, confidence and time are sent.
  • Offline-first: every report is stored in a local Room database first and synced by WorkManager when the network returns, idempotently.
  • Smart de-duplication: repeated passes update one pothole record; each pass is kept as an immutable detection for evidence.
  • Live Companion Mode: others can follow a scanning vehicle's position and detections in real time — without video.
  • Open, free mapping: OpenStreetMap / OpenFreeMap tiles, so there are no map API keys or billing.

4Methodology

The work follows an iterative engineering methodology: build a working baseline, measure it against defined metrics, and improve the weakest component in each cycle. Four technical streams run in parallel.

4.1 Detection model

The baseline is a single-class YOLOv8s detector (11.1 M parameters, 28.4 GFLOPs at 640×640) obtained as a public pretrained checkpoint. It is exported to ONNX and dynamically quantized to INT8, reducing size from 42.67 MB to 10.96 MB. Input is a [1, 3, 640, 640] normalised tensor; output is [1, 5, 8400] candidate boxes, filtered at confidence 0.25 and non-max suppression IoU 0.45. Because the baseline was not trained on local roads and no labelled validation set exists yet, this project will collect and annotate a local dataset (varied lighting, rain, road types, camera heights), fine-tune the model, and compare baseline and fine-tuned versions on a held-out test split.

4.2 On-device decision pipeline

StageRulePurpose
Temporal confirmationPothole present in 2 of 3 consecutive analysed framesRemove single-frame false positives
Report gatesConfidence ≥ 0.55; 3.5 s cooldown; 10 m / 60 s same-spot gateAvoid weak and repeated reports
SeverityBox-area ratio and confidence → LOW / MEDIUM / HIGHPrioritise repairs
Location qualityGPS fix ≤ 30 s old and ≤ 50 m accuracy, else not reportedNever file a report at a guessed position
Driver warningVoice + haptic; "ahead" if within 65° of headingImmediate, offline safety benefit

4.3 Data synchronisation and de-duplication

Each confirmed detection receives a client UUID and is written to the Room outbox. A unique WorkManager job uploads pending rows with exponential backoff. On the server side, a Firebase transaction searches a bounded latitude band and merges the detection into an existing pothole within clamp(GPS accuracy, 10 m, 25 m), otherwise it creates a new report. This keeps one record per physical defect while preserving every pass as evidence.

4.4 Workflow, security and governance

Reports move through nine stages — NEW → UNDER_REVIEW → VERIFIED → ASSIGNED → ACCEPTED → IN_PROGRESS → REPAIR_SUBMITTED → CITY_VERIFICATION → RESOLVED — with rejection, declined-assignment and repair-rejected branches. Firebase security rules, not the user interface, decide who may perform each transition: self-registration always creates a citizen, contractors can act only on their own jobs, only the city can close a report, and every change appends an attributed entry to activity_logs/. The rules are verified by automated emulator tests.

Ethics and privacy

  • Privacy by design: no image or video is uploaded during normal use; only detection metadata is stored.
  • Dataset collection: recorded frames are used only for training; faces and number plates will be blurred or excluded before any sharing.
  • Data minimisation and access: detections are private to the reporter; profiles and live locations are visible only to authorised roles or session participants.
  • Safety: the app is used only with a mounted phone, and alerts never require the driver to touch the screen.
  • Consent: pilot participants will be informed of what is recorded and may withdraw at any time.

11Conclusion

SmartPothole AI combines edge AI, offline-first mobile engineering and a secure, auditable workflow into one practical system for road maintenance. The prototype shows that the architecture works; the proposed 16-week plan will measure how well it works on local roads and real phones, close the remaining feature gaps, and deliver a validated system and dataset that a city authority could adopt.
